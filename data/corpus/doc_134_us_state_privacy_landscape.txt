US state privacy landscape for health data: The United States has no single federal privacy law like the GDPR. HIPAA covers only covered entities and business associates, so consumer health apps, wearables, data brokers and many websites sit outside it. HIPAA sets a federal floor: state laws that are more stringent, giving individuals greater privacy protection or rights, are not preempted (45 CFR 160.203). Layers that apply in practice: comprehensive state consumer privacy laws, such as those of California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut and Texas, which treat health data as sensitive data requiring opt-in consent or a right to limit, but typically exempt HIPAA PHI; state health-information laws, such as California's CMIA and Texas HB 300; consumer health data laws, such as Washington's My Health My Data Act and Nevada's SB 370; and state breach notification laws, which exist in all 50 states and usually have shorter or different clocks than HIPAA. Special categories receive extra statutes in many states: HIV status, mental health, substance use disorder records (also 42 CFR Part 2), genetic information and reproductive health. The FTC applies s. 5 of the FTC Act and its Health Breach Notification Rule to non-HIPAA health data.
