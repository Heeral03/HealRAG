UK common law duty of confidentiality in health care: Information given in confidence, including patient information held by clinicians, generally may not be disclosed without the individual's consent, a legal duty such as a court order or statute, or an overriding public interest. Implied consent: for the provision of direct care, the National Data Guardian and the Caldicott Principles accept that patients reasonably expect relevant information to be shared among the health and care professionals who are involved in their care, so explicit consent is not needed, provided the patient has been informed and has not objected. Sharing for purposes beyond direct care, such as research, planning and commissioning, generally needs consent, a s. 251 approval, or the use of anonymised data; the National Data Opt-out applies to most such uses of confidential patient information. Public interest disclosures, for example to prevent serious crime or serious harm, are assessed case by case. The duty of confidentiality continues after death. Caldicott Guardians in each organisation advise on these decisions. Note that the legal basis for processing under the UK GDPR is separate from the common law.
