GDPR Chapter V - Transfers of personal data to third countries or international organisations (Articles 44 to 49): Any transfer may take place only if the conditions of Ch. V are complied with by the controller and processor, so that the level of protection guaranteed by the Regulation is not undermined. Art. 45 - adequacy decisions: a transfer may take place where the European Commission has decided that the third country, territory or sector ensures an adequate level of protection. No specific authorisation is needed. Art. 46 - appropriate safeguards: in the absence of an adequacy decision, a transfer may take place subject to enforceable data subject rights and effective legal remedies through standard contractual clauses adopted by the Commission, binding corporate rules (Art. 47), approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms, and other listed instruments. Following the Schrems II judgment, exporters must assess whether the third country's laws undermine the safeguards and add supplementary measures where needed. Art. 49 - derogations for specific situations: explicit consent after being informed of the risks; necessity for a contract; important reasons of public interest; legal claims; vital interests of the data subject who is physically or legally incapable of giving consent; and public registers. These derogations are interpreted restrictively.
