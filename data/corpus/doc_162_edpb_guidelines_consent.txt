EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent: Valid consent under the GDPR must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, given by a statement or clear affirmative action. Freely given: there must be real choice and control; consent is not freely given if there is a clear imbalance of power, such as between a public authority or employer and an individual, or if consent is bundled as a condition for a service. Specific: consent must be tied to a defined purpose; granularity is needed where there are several purposes. Informed: the data subject must know at least the controller's identity, each purpose, the types of data, the right to withdraw, use for automated decisions and transfer risks. Unambiguous: pre-ticked boxes and silence or inactivity are not consent. Explicit consent, required for special category data under Art. 9(2)(a), needs an express statement of consent, for example a written statement or a two-stage verification. The Guidelines note that in clinical research, consent under the GDPR as a lawful basis is distinct from informed consent to participate in a clinical trial, and that scientific research may rely on broader consent to areas of research when ethical standards are respected.
