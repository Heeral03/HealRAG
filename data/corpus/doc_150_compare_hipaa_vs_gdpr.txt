HIPAA versus GDPR for health data, a summary comparison. Scope: HIPAA applies only to covered entities (health plans, clearinghouses, providers using standard electronic transactions) and their business associates, and protects PHI; the GDPR applies to every controller and processor processing personal data of people in the EU, and treats data concerning health as a special category under Art. 9. Legal basis: HIPAA permits uses and disclosures for treatment, payment and health care operations without consent and requires authorization for most other purposes; the GDPR requires a lawful basis under Art. 6 and, for health data, a condition under Art. 9(2), such as explicit consent, healthcare provision under Art. 9(2)(h), or public health under Art. 9(2)(i). Breach notification: HIPAA allows up to 60 calendar days to notify individuals and HHS; the GDPR requires notifying the supervisory authority within 72 hours where feasible (Art. 33) and individuals without undue delay when there is high risk (Art. 34). De-identification versus anonymisation: HIPAA has a rules-based Safe Harbor and Expert Determination; the GDPR sets a risk-based test of whether a person is identifiable by means reasonably likely to be used, and pseudonymised data remains personal data. Individual rights: HIPAA grants access, amendment, accounting of disclosures and restriction; the GDPR adds erasure, portability, objection and rights concerning automated decisions. Penalties: HIPAA civil penalties follow culpability tiers, adjusted annually; GDPR fines reach EUR 20 million or 4 percent of worldwide annual turnover. Enforcement: HHS OCR for HIPAA; national supervisory authorities for the GDPR, with a private right of action for damages under GDPR Art. 82 but not under HIPAA.
