Texas Medical Records Privacy Act (HB 300), Health and Safety Code Ch. 181: Applies HIPAA-like duties to a broader group than federal HIPAA by defining covered entity to include any person who comes into possession of, obtains, stores or assembles protected health information, including employees and contractors of health care providers, data processors and many non-clinical businesses. Requirements include: training of employees on Texas and federal privacy law within 90 days of hiring and at least every two years; notice to patients that their PHI is subject to electronic disclosure; a prohibition on selling PHI; a consumer right to receive records in electronic form within 15 business days of a request; and authorisation for most marketing communications. Texas breach law (Business and Commerce Code Ch. 521) separately requires notice to affected individuals without unreasonable delay and within 60 days of discovery, and notice to the Attorney General within 30 days if 250 or more Texas residents are affected. Civil penalties scale with culpability and are enforced by the Attorney General and licensing agencies, with higher penalties for knowing or intentional violations and for conduct for financial gain.
