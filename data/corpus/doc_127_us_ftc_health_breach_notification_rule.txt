FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, 16 CFR Part 318: Applies to vendors of personal health records, PHR-related entities and their third-party service providers that are not covered by HIPAA, including many health and wellness apps, connected devices and websites. The 2024 amendments, effective 29 July 2024, clarify that the rule covers health apps and similar technologies; that a breach of security includes unauthorised disclosure, such as sharing health data with advertisers without the user's authorisation, not only a cyberattack; and that PHR identifiable health information includes data drawn from multiple sources, including a user's device. After discovering a breach, a covered entity must notify affected individuals and the FTC without unreasonable delay and within 60 calendar days; for breaches affecting 500 or more people the FTC must be notified at the same time as individuals, and for fewer than 500 within the same 60-day window through an annual log. Media notice is required if more than 500 residents of a state are affected. Violations are treated as unfair or deceptive acts under the FTC Act, with civil penalties per violation. The FTC has used the rule against GoodRx and Premom, among others. FTC Act s. 5 also applies to deceptive health privacy claims.
