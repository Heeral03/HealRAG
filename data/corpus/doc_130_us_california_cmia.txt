California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA), Civil Code s. 56 et seq.: Prohibits providers of health care, health care service plans and contractors from disclosing medical information about a patient without a valid authorization, except for listed purposes such as treatment, payment, and as required by law. It is broader than HIPAA in several ways: it applies to any business that offers software or hardware, including mobile apps, designed to maintain medical information so a consumer can manage their information or receive diagnosis, treatment or management of a condition, and treats such businesses as providers of health care. Amendments extended the law to reproductive and sexual health digital services and to mental health application information. CMIA provides a private right of action: an individual may sue for nominal damages of $1,000 for a negligent release of medical information without proof of actual harm, plus actual damages, and administrative fines and civil penalties are available for knowing or willful violations. Information that is HIPAA PHI held by a HIPAA covered entity is largely exempt from the CCPA, but not from the CMIA.
