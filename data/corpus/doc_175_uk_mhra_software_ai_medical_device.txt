MHRA regulation of software and AI as a medical device in Great Britain: Software is a medical device if the manufacturer intends it for a medical purpose such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease. It is regulated under the Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (SI 2002/618), as amended. Manufacturers must determine the device class, apply a UKCA mark (CE marking is currently also accepted for a transition period) through a UK Approved Body for higher classes, register the device with the MHRA, and operate post-market surveillance, including vigilance reporting of serious incidents. The Software and AI as a Medical Device Change Programme sets out work to provide clearer guidance and requirements, including on predetermined change control plans, good machine learning practice (aligned with FDA and Health Canada principles) and transparency, and the MHRA AI Airlock regulatory sandbox for testing AI as a medical device. The programme parallels the EU MDR and AI Act in Europe and FDA oversight in the United States.
