HIPAA penalty tiers: Civil money penalties under 45 CFR 160.404 and 42 USC 1320d-5 are set in four tiers according to culpability: (1) the entity did not know and, by exercising reasonable diligence, would not have known of the violation; (2) reasonable cause, but not willful neglect; (3) willful neglect that was corrected within 30 days; (4) willful neglect that was not corrected. Each tier has a minimum and maximum amount per violation and an annual cap for identical violations; the amounts are adjusted for inflation each year, and HHS applies lower annual caps for the lower tiers under its 2019 notice of enforcement discretion. The HITECH Act extended these penalties to business associates and required HHS to investigate complaints involving willful neglect. State attorneys general may also bring civil actions for HIPAA violations. Criminal penalties under 42 USC 1320d-6, enforced by the Department of Justice: knowingly obtaining or disclosing PHI in violation of HIPAA carries up to $50,000 and one year in prison; offences committed under false pretences carry up to $100,000 and five years; offences committed with intent to sell, transfer or use PHI for commercial advantage, personal gain or malicious harm carry up to $250,000 and ten years. OCR commonly resolves cases through resolution agreements with corrective action plans, typically running two to three years, as well as civil money penalties.
