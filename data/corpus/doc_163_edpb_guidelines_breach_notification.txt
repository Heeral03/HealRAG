EDPB Guidelines 9/2022 on personal data breach notification: A personal data breach is a breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data. Breaches are classified as confidentiality, integrity or availability breaches, and a temporary loss of availability, such as a hospital system being unavailable, can itself be a breach. A controller is considered 'aware' when it has a reasonable degree of certainty that a security incident has occurred that has led to personal data being compromised; the 72-hour clock under Art. 33 starts then. The controller assesses risk using the type of breach, nature, sensitivity and volume of data, ease of identification, severity of consequences, special characteristics of the individual, and number of affected individuals. Health data breaches usually pose a high risk because of potential discrimination, identity theft and physical or reputational harm. Where a controller is not established in the EU and has no lead supervisory authority under the one-stop-shop mechanism, it must notify each supervisory authority in the Member States where affected individuals reside. Ransomware that causes loss of availability, with a backup restored promptly, may still require notification depending on risk.
