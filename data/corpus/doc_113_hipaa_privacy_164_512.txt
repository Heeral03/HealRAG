HIPAA Privacy Rule, 45 CFR 164.512 (uses and disclosures for which authorization or an opportunity to agree or object is not required): A covered entity may use or disclose PHI without the individual's authorization for twelve public-interest purposes, subject to conditions. They are: required by law; public health activities (preventing or controlling disease, reporting births and deaths, child abuse reporting, FDA-regulated product safety, and notifying people exposed to a communicable disease); victims of abuse, neglect or domestic violence; health oversight activities; judicial and administrative proceedings; law enforcement purposes; decedents (coroners, medical examiners, funeral directors); organ and tissue donation; research; serious threats to health or safety; specialised government functions; and workers' compensation. Research (164.512(i)): PHI may be used for research without authorization if an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Privacy Board approves a waiver or alteration of authorization, if the use is preparatory to research, or if it concerns research on decedents. The waiver criteria require that the use involves no more than minimal privacy risk, that the research could not practicably be done without the waiver, and that it could not practicably be done without the PHI. Most 164.512 disclosures are subject to the minimum necessary standard.
