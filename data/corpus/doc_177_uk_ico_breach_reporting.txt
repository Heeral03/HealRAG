ICO guidance on personal data breaches under the UK GDPR: Controllers must report a notifiable personal data breach to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms. If the breach is likely to result in a high risk, such as disclosure of health or medical records, the controller must also tell the affected individuals without undue delay. A report is made through the ICO's online form or helpline. NHS organisations and other health and care providers using the Data Security and Protection Toolkit report incidents through the DSPT incident reporting tool, which sends the report to the ICO, NHS England and the Department of Health and Social Care as appropriate. Controllers must keep a record of all breaches, including those not reported, and be able to show the reasoning. Processors must tell controllers without undue delay. Failure to report a notifiable breach can attract a fixed penalty of up to GBP 8.7 million or 2 percent of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; the higher maximum of GBP 17.5 million or 4 percent applies to infringements of the principles and data subject rights. This corresponds to HIPAA's 60-day deadline in the US.
