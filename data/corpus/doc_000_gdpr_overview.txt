Overview - What is the GDPR: The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Regulation (EU) 2016/679, is the European Union law on the protection of personal data and privacy. It was adopted on 27 April 2016, has applied since 25 May 2018, and replaced Directive 95/46/EC. As a regulation it is directly applicable in all EU Member States. It governs how controllers and processors collect, use, store and share the personal data of people in the EU, and it also applies to organisations outside the EU that offer goods or services to, or monitor the behaviour of, people in the EU (Art. 3). Core elements: data protection principles such as lawfulness, purpose limitation and data minimisation (Art. 5); lawful bases for processing (Art. 6); stricter rules for special category data including health data (Art. 9); data subject rights such as access, rectification and erasure (Arts. 12-23); security of processing (Art. 32); and personal data breach notification (Arts. 33-34). Supervisory authorities enforce it, with administrative fines of up to EUR 20 million or 4 percent of worldwide annual turnover (Art. 83). After Brexit the UK retained the regulation as the UK GDPR, supplemented by the Data Protection Act 2018, and the ICO is the UK regulator. This is a summary for orientation; the binding text is in the individual Articles.
