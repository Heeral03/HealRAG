Overview - What is HIPAA: The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA, Public Law 104-191) is the United States federal law that protects the privacy and security of individually identifiable health information. Its implementing rules are in 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164: the Privacy Rule (Part 164 Subpart E), the Security Rule (Part 164 Subpart C), the Breach Notification Rule (Part 164 Subpart D) and the Enforcement Rule (Part 160). HIPAA applies to covered entities (health plans, health care clearinghouses and health care providers that transmit health information electronically in standard transactions) and to their business associates. The protected information is called protected health information (PHI): individually identifiable health information held or transmitted by a covered entity or business associate in any form. The HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) enforces HIPAA. The HITECH Act of 2009 strengthened it with breach notification duties, direct liability for business associates and higher penalties. Unlike the GDPR, HIPAA is sectoral: it does not cover every organisation that holds health data, so health apps and other non-covered entities fall under other laws such as the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state privacy laws. This is a summary for orientation; the binding text is in 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164.
