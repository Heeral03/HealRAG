Information blocking, 21st Century Cures Act s.4004 and 45 CFR Part 171: Information blocking is a practice that is likely to interfere with access, exchange or use of electronic health information (EHI), except as required by law or covered by an exception. It applies to three types of actors: health care providers; health IT developers of certified health IT; and health information exchanges and health information networks. For providers, the practice must be one the provider knows is unreasonable and likely to interfere; for developers, exchanges and networks, they know or should know it is likely to interfere. Exceptions fall into those that involve not fulfilling a request (preventing harm, privacy, security, infeasibility, health IT performance) and those that involve procedures for fulfilling a request (content and manner, fees, licensing). The 2024 HTI-1 final rule added further exceptions, including one for TEFCA manner. Penalties differ by actor: developers, exchanges and networks face civil money penalties of up to $1 million per violation, enforced by the HHS Office of Inspector General; providers face appropriate disincentives set by CMS programmes, such as loss of Medicare Promoting Interoperability credit. Since 2022 the EHI covered is all electronic protected health information in a designated record set, not just the USCDI data elements.
